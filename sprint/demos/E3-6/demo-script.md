# Demo Script — E3-6

**Setup before demo:** Start the Gamebox server locally with `npm start` and navigate to the Sorry! game. Have a game in progress against The Bully (red) persona, with at least one pawn out on the board.

---

**Scene 1 — The Board (Slide 3: What We Built) · 0:00–0:45**

Open the browser to the Sorry! game URL. Point to the board image.

> "This is the full Sorry! board — the ring track, both players' Safety lanes and Homes, and the three Slides. It's rendered from a single pre-drawn image generated by a Python script. Every cell maps 1:1 to the game engine's coordinate system."

Point to the pawn tokens sitting on the board.

> "These four green pawns are mine; the four red ones belong to The Bully. Their positions come directly from the server — the browser just places them."

**Fallback:** If the app isn't loading, show Slide 3's screenshot of the board.

---

**Scene 2 — Drawing a Card (Slide 3 continued) · 0:45–1:30**

The deck should be visible face-down. Click it.

> "Watch what happens when I draw. The card flips. I drew a 7 — that means I can move one pawn seven spaces, or split the seven across two pawns."

After the flip, point to the highlighted move targets on the board.

> "Every legal move I can make lights up automatically. The server calculated all of these; my browser is just showing them."

**Fallback:** Show a pre-captured screenshot of the card-flip state.

---

**Scene 3 — Making a Move (Slide 3 continued) · 1:30–2:15**

Click one of the highlighted move targets.

> "I click the square I want. The pawn moves there. No confirmation dialog, no submit button — single click commits the action. The move was sent to the server, validated, applied, and the new state came back."

**Fallback:** If the click fails, describe the flow: "Click → POST to server → engine validates → new state → browser re-renders."

---

**Scene 4 — The AI Takes Its Turn (Slide 3 continued) · 2:15–3:00**

After your move, wait a moment. The AI opponent's pawn should move.

> "I didn't do anything — that was The Bully making its move. The AI orchestrator woke up, saw it was the bot's turn, picked a move based on The Bully's aggressive strategy, and applied it. The human player only ever sees a result."

> "There are two AI personalities: The Bully prioritizes knocking your pawns back to Start. The Tortoise plays it safe and methodical. Same engine, same rules, completely different feel."

**Fallback:** If the AI move isn't visible, show Slide 4 (Why This Approach) and explain the orchestrator contract.

---

**Scene 5 — Win Banner (Slide 3 continued) · 3:00–3:30**

If a win state is available in a pre-staged game, trigger it. Otherwise describe it.

> "When the last pawn reaches Home, this banner appears. It's keyed directly to the server's `winner` field — the client never decides who won, it only reads the result."

**Command to force-show a pre-staged win state (if needed):**
```bash
# Load the pre-staged won-game fixture in a second browser tab
open http://localhost:3000/sorry?debug=win-fixture
```

**Fallback:** Screenshot of the win banner on Slide 3.
